Retail Co · Case study

Empowering item discovery with Gen AI summaries

Shoppers were drowning in reviews and spec tables. We designed an AI summary that answers the real question — “is this right for me?” — at a glance.

Role
Co-lead designer
Responsibilities
Research, interaction & visual design, design-system contribution
Team
2 designers, PM, 6 engineers, data science, content
Timeline
Q3 2023 · ~14 weeks

The challenge

The item page had grown into a wall of content: 40+ spec rows, thousands of reviews, and a rating number with no context. In usability sessions, shoppers scrolled past all of it and left to compare on other sites. Business impact was measurable — item pages with heavy review volume had lower add-to-cart rates than lighter ones.

Two problems in one

  • Item page overload. No hierarchy between “need to know” and “nice to know.”
  • Ratings without meaning. A 4.3 average told shoppers nothing about why.

Kickoff

We ran a two-day in-person workshop with 20+ partner teams to map the journey and agree on where a summary could help. We split the space into three moments: awareness, consideration, and decision — and focused the MVP on consideration and decision.

Research

A competitive teardown across retail and non-retail products (marketplaces, review sites, maps, meeting tools) surfaced two themes that shaped the whole design:

  • Essentials to know. The best summaries lead with 3–5 concrete takeaways, not prose.
  • Trust & transparency. People trust AI output more when it shows its sources and stays honest about uncertainty.

AI is making our lives easier — tasks feel more streamlined.

Participant, unmoderated study

But is this actually a person that reviewed this?

Participant, unmoderated study

Ideation

Four rounds, each testing a different bet: summary as a card, as an expandable band, as a chat entry point, and as inline highlights woven through existing content. We converged on a card with a fixed structure — a one-line verdict, a short list of pros/cons drawn from reviews, and a “what people mention most” cluster.

User testing

Unmoderated testing with 60 participants, comparing the card against the current page. Key learnings:

  • Percentage match language felt overconfident — we softened it to qualitative phrasing.
  • Item specs in the summary were the most-cited reason people felt ready to decide.
  • Review summaries needed a visible “based on 1,240 reviews” line to be believed.

Design decisions

We explored a dark, distinct “AI zone” treatment versus a light module that matched the rest of the page. The dark version drew the eye but tested as “an ad.” We shipped the light treatment — familiar, trusted, and still visually anchored by a small AI marker.

Final design

The MVP shipped across search results, the item page, and a lightweight compare view. The summary component became a documented pattern in the design system, with tokens for the AI marker and rules for sourcing lines.

Impact

+2.62%Add-to-cart conversion (item page)
−1.81%Cart removals
1 patternAdded to the design system

What I'd do differently: we under-invested in the empty state for items with few reviews. It shipped as a fast follow, but it should have been in the first cut.
