IFS Cloud · Case study

Smart Editor: AI writing help, built once and used everywhere

An AI writing assistant added to IFS Cloud's central framework, so every product group — HCM, finance, supply chain, projects — can generate, rewrite and summarise content inside their own screens. Core release shipped in 25R1.

Role
Lead product designer
Focus
Interaction design, AI UX, accessibility, validation
Platform
IFS Cloud — central framework, used by all product groups
Release
25R1 shipped · 25R2 advanced mode next

Context

IFS is a large ERP platform with many product areas — projects, finance, supply chain, HCM and more. Every one of them has people writing text: job descriptions, forecast reports, notes, summaries.

Smart Editor was introduced as a shared tool in the central framework, so those groups can use AI writing help in their own apps instead of each building their own. HCM, for example, can configure it to write a job description. It was one of the biggest projects I've led, and I worked on it as its designer.

One tool, many jobs

Because it lives in the framework, the same pattern has to work for very different content and users. Smart Editor is used to:

  • Generate daily forecast reports for supply chain
  • Draft job descriptions
  • Correct grammar
  • Summarise long reports
  • Generate content from given metadata

That was the design challenge: an interaction flexible enough for every product group, and light enough that it never gets in the way of someone who is just writing.

Starting with what people already use

I began by looking at the writing assistants people already know — Confluence, Notion and a range of Chrome extensions — and asked how they behave and how they feel to use.

How it works

The interaction is two small widgets, so the writer stays in their document.

1 — Select text, choose an action

When a user selects text, a compact widget appears next to it. From there they can summarise, modify or generate content.

2 — Review the result, then decide

Choosing an action opens a second widget with the result. The user can replace their original text with it, or insert it below and keep both.

3 — Advanced mode, when a quick action isn't enough

Advanced Smart Editor opens in a drawer with more control: history, and toggles under “AI Inputs” to customise the answer — for example switching Basic Information, Job Certification or Job Format on and off. A single click moves the user from the quick widget into it, keeping their text and context. It also opens on its own when the text is too long for the compact widget.

Design decisions

Dynamic suggestions

Highlighting a phrase or sentence gives tailored suggestions for that text, without breaking the writer's flow.

Partial text assistance

Users can select just part of the text for AI help, so the rest of their content is never rewritten by accident.

Fits any text field

It works in note fields, multi-text inputs and other content-editing fields, so product groups can add it wherever it's useful.

A single click into advanced mode

Users move to detailed configuration in one step, without starting over (see the advanced mode screens above).

Accessibility from the start

  • A distinct “glitter pen” button marks every input and note field that has Smart Editor enabled.
  • Tooltip guidance on the button helps people activate it.
  • Keyboard access: the editor can be opened without a mouse, using a shortcut such as Ctrl+E.
  • Inclusive design: people with different levels of technical confidence should be able to use it intuitively.

Built to be reused: two tiers of “recipes”

Behind the interface, Smart Editor has two tiers, so product teams can adapt it without redesigning it:

  • Product group recipes — configurable, use-case-specific features, such as writing a job summary.
  • Framework recipes — shared core features such as grammar correction, embedded across modules and customised per field.

Designing around real people

I used short stories to keep the work grounded, starting with Maya, a recruiting officer at a large company who writes job summaries and descriptions to attract talent. The design was validated with HCM.

Two more scenarios shaped the flows:

  • Job description with fine control. A hiring manager asks Maya for a QA engineer job description, using information already in IFS but without the certification requirements. Advanced mode lets her switch those attributes off.
  • Notes to structured text. Nina in HR Talent Acquisition has rough meeting notes and no time to write up activities for a performance evaluation page. Smart Editor turns the notes into clear content.

Validation

I validated the design in five steps, each feeding the next round of iteration:

  • Usability validation — an internal test with HCM members to confirm the use case and check the design.
  • Stakeholder demo — shown at the UX/AI demo, where UX designers and product specialists shared ideas.
  • Heuristic validation — checked against IFS usability standards.
  • End-user validation — planned after refining the design with the feedback above (in progress).
  • Expert review — held weekly on the final designs, with product designers' input to be added.

Release plan

25R1 — released

Core functionality: users select full or partial text in supported fields with the mouse, and the AI spark button opens a compact pop-up with grammar fixes and improved text. They can accept a suggestion, discard it, or rewrite and regenerate.

25R2 — next

  • Advanced mode with side-by-side comparison of original and AI-generated text, toggles for content attributes such as page information or certification data, and full manual editing of the AI output.
  • Custom recipes that product teams define per use case, such as job summaries or feedback.
  • Content generation from empty fields, so users can get a first draft or summary even when nothing is written yet.
