Rebuilding onboarding to get teams to value on day one

New workspaces had to clear 14 setup steps before they could do anything useful. Most never finished. We rebuilt it around what actually signals activation.

Role
Lead designer
Responsibilities
Journey mapping, prototyping, UX writing, A/B test design
Team
1 designer, PM, 4 engineers, lifecycle marketing
Timeline
Q1 2024 · ~10 weeks

The challenge

The wizard forced every new workspace through the same linear path — invite teammates, connect integrations, configure settings — before showing the product. Funnel data told the story: 61% of workspaces dropped before step 6, and the ones that did finish weren't more likely to stick.

I just want to see if this thing works before I go bug my whole team to sign up.

Trial admin, discovery interview

Reframe

We stopped treating setup as a gate. Working with data, we defined three activation moments that actually predicted retention — create a project, complete one real task in it, and invite a second person — and designed everything around reaching those, fast.

Design

  • Drop into the product immediately with a sample project already populated.
  • A persistent checklist in the corner — progress you can see, steps you can skip or snooze.
  • Contextual nudges instead of modals: the invite prompt appears when you'd naturally want a teammate.

Testing

Prototype tests with 12 trial admins, then a staged A/B test at 50/50. We watched setup completion, time to first real task, and week-4 retention as a guardrail.

Impact

+38%Setup completion
−22%Time to first project
+6ptWeek-4 retention (activated cohort)

What I'd do differently: the mobile checklist shipped as a compressed version of desktop. It needed its own layout from the start.
